Estimate Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR)

Use this application to estimate the contrast-to-noise ratio from a list of comma-separated values representing a one-dimensional excited state population profile.

You do not need to subtract the background or normalize the profile. The estimate does not change when the values are multiplied by a constant or shifted by a constant. For a reliable estimate:

  1. enter at least 16 values, and
  2. include the flat background on both sides of the peak. The baseline is estimated from the outer quarter of the profile on each side.

Enter your noisy Gaussian profile as a list of comma-separated values here:

The CNR estimate comes from the fft-cnr package. In outline:

  1. The profile is transformed into frequency space, where the signal occupies the low frequencies and white noise spreads over all frequencies.
  2. The boundary frequency between signal and noise is chosen with the Akaike information criterion (AIC).
  3. The root mean squared (RMS) noise is estimated from the frequencies above that boundary.
  4. The CNR is the height of the signal peak above the baseline divided by the RMS noise.

The fft-cnr documentation describes the method and its limits.
